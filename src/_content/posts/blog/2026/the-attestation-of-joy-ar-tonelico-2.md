Explaining the essence of Ar tonelico is fairly difficult. I will not lie to you and tell you every game is for everyone. However, when Ar tonelico clicks for you, it irrevocably alters your brain chemistry. You witnessed the best thing ever and there is nothing else like it except other EXA_PICO games. And you weep, for there are only five of them and the series is dead.

Back in 2007, I very much enjoyed Ar tonelico 1 as my first Gust game. It's a special game to me to this day. Yet the game I love above all others, the one I keep coming back to, the absolute masterpiece of the lot, beyond a doubt is Ar tonelico 2.

I loved it before it had a good translation. I loved it after its first retranslation. And I love it even more with its second retranslation.

To be clear, I am under the impression that a lot of the PS2 Gust games need a retranslation. When I was playing Ar tonelico 1 earlier this year, I caught a lot of issues just by listening to the Japanese voices. To say that NISA did a poor job is an understatement. I've witnessed a number of issues in other games, but not as thoroughly as AT1.

AT2 had an *infamously bad* translation, so bad that the NISA President apologized for it. If you think to play the game as NISA released it: don't. You are not getting what AT2 is intended to be, you're getting the butchered version. There are TWO retranslations, and that is for good reason.

The version you SHOULD experience is at the <a href="https://www.projectmetafalica.com/" target="_blank">Project Metafalica website.</a>

## Now what the heck is this?

The Ar tonelico games are works by Akira Tsuchiya. He joined Gust in its early days as a composer and took on other roles in the company. In 2000, he pitched a game based on his tabletop RPG setting. This pitch was rejected, but he later pitched a modified version to Banpresto (which was later acquired by Bandai Namco), and this is what formed into Ar tonelico.

A question the first game asked is "What is it to understand someone?"

The second takes that question and beats you over the head with understanding. It systematically realizes complete understanding of the leading ladies upon you. Especially the terrible parts they don't want you to know, and you're gonna love it. And them.

Not only are you gonna understand every last detail of the leading ladies, you're gonna get massive amounts of understanding toward the extended cast. Every conflict has a reason; over the course of the story, you'll see most of these explained and several even resolved.

And I do think it's noteworthy that there are characters who are left more open to interpretation. The most obvious is the protagonist. He has so many obvious missing details that you hunger for more, but we'll never see it all.

So how does AT2 tackle understanding each other? Multiple ways.

I baited you, because this actually requires more explanation before I start. You see, there's a lot of cool lore. You can engage with it as deeply or as shallowly as you want. There's an entire Setting Book (translated on the wiki!) filled with all the details the game never gives you. There's also an in-game glossary that will explain many things; the terms even get a fun highlight in the dialog when one is added. There is a lot of supplemental lore that is given to us for love of the game, history and science both.

Ar tonelico is a sci-fi series about a nearly doomed world. The planet is covered in a literal Sea of Death and humanity clings to the pieces of land that float above it. Ar tonelico 2 takes place in the region of Metafalss, a struggling land that has almost lost all hope. The people there wish to realize the legend of Metafalica, a utopic, fertile land created through the power of Song, but the Goddess has denied them this hope multiple times.

At the center of this problem are our romantic interests: the Holy Maiden, Cloche Leythal Pastalie, as well as the popular girl-next-door Dive Therapist, Luca Trulywaath. They are a special race known as Reyvateils. All Reyvateils are women, and you can bet there's a silly lore reason why it's impossible for men. Reyvateils can use powerful Song Magic to both heal and attack. Song Magic is essentially the conversion of emotion into energy.

So how do we create Song Magic? Well, have you ever wanted to see the inside of someone's mind? To have it laid bare into nine parts, increasingly deeper into the subconscious? I can't say I ever wanted that, but it quickly became something I had to have when Ar tonelico first delivered it to me.

You evoke strong feelings by Diving into a Reyvateil's mental world, known as the Cosmosphere, creating stronger magic in the process. The first layers are shallow, covering issues the girl is conscious of. As the protagonist, you nudge her into sorting out her feelings on a number of problems and get into crazier and crazier, more subconscious feelings. You get to *truly* know her, and going deeper will eventually establish a real romantic relationship with her. No, you can't two-time her, our protagonist Croix Bartel is a good boy.

Cloche is a classic tsundere, but how did she even twist herself into being one? Obviously her status prevents her from being straightforward with others; she practically bears the weight of the world on her shoulders.

Luca's wide popularity doesn't come without significant effort. What sort of person is capable of being liked by everybody? And what does she keep hidden behind that smile she gives to everyone?

Dive to find out more!

Incidentally, Luca is Croix's girlfriend at the start of the game, she's even preparing to move in with him. How is Cloche a romantic option at all when this is the case?

They are uniquely terrible women and I hope you come to love them as I have.

When I said you get to know the leading ladies, I didn't just mean through the lens of Croix. You will see Cloche and Luca as they see each other as well. It goes dark and ugly, and doesn't deal with their deep subconscious at all. They annoy the hell out of each other, but we also bear witness to how they come to love each other.

Ar tonelico 2 is not a subtle game. Somehow getting beaten over the head with every bit of understanding never gets tiresome. If you play this game twice, you get the pleasure of being amazed at what it boldly holds under your nose. Be prepared to sniff out some things on your own. And be prepared to be bamboozled. Phase 1 is **dense** with the info it feeds you… and I've been doing my best to not tell you about anything that lies beyond it.

There are five phases to the game. I think the weakest is Phase 2 because it hits the brakes on the story, though it is still rich with information. It's full speed ahead after that. The absolute HIGHS that it's setting up for cannot be understated.

On its face, Ar tonelico as a whole is incredibly *goofy*. You are meant to laugh, and laugh a lot. It's also incredibly Japanese. These traits combined tend to have people write it off as some silly Japanese game, and I think that's a shame. Let it be known: however silly something seems, there is something serious underneath it. Except for the shop synthesis, that's just gags. Shop synthesis is very worth doing, by the way, so be sure to add a few hours to your playtime by doing them all. You'll get sincere stories about the shopkeeps between the item creation gags.

I've told you about the story, but there's another important element I haven't mentioned yet: the music. Yeah, Tsuchiya is a musician, did you think his *baby* wouldn't have good music?

But it goes SO FAR BEYOND.

There is, of course, the regular OST. In this respect, I think its predecessor has it beat, but it's a hell of a good OST. Even if the song 'Warmth' is quite overused. (It's the feel good slow song. You will hear it more times on Cloche's route, but this is slightly cheating because it's mostly her final Cosmosphere stratum, which accounts for about a tenth of the total plays of 'Warmth'. I COUNTED OKAY. ENJOY THE USELESS TRIVIA.)

What truly makes Ar tonelico special is the vocal tracks. It's a style you can *unmistakably* identify as 'Ar tonelico'.

Remember how the leading ladies are Reyvateils and use Song Magic? They also sing much grander Hymmnos Songs, which have greater story-related functions than normal Song Magic. Hymmnos is also the name of the constructed language that is featured in these songs. It features in a few regular OST tracks, it features in multiple major songs, and it features in a lot of supplemental material as well, such as the drama CDs. It is a proper language, with real grammar, and it is used in the game multiple times. Remember when I said you can engage with the lore as shallowly or deeply as you want? The depths include making your own fan songs in Hymmnos.

Not every major song is a Hymmnos Song, by the way, but this doesn't imply they are less impactful in their usage. The major songs with no Hymmnos at all are just as worthy of your attention.

When you beat the game, you would do well to look up the lyrics. However deeply or shallowly you engage with the rest of the lore, please know the stories that the songs truly tell.

It's genuinely hard to not like Tsuchiya as a creator. For years he (alongside writer Genki Tomimatsu) ran a column answering fan questions about the games, usually as characters in the series. So, yes, people got their waifu to answer their questions in the 'Toukousphere'. It's rare to engage with fans on that level… and have the depth of lore and love for characters to back that up.

And even after all this, plainly, you do not understand. Ar tonelico is one of a kind.

## What about the first game?

Much as I love 1, it's hard to speak of it with the same adoration that I give to its sequel. Knowing the translation is bad makes it harder to recommend.

Play the second. I won't let you escape, I can see you trying. Play the second game. The first game can wait until *after* if you're dying for more, but the second is largely self-contained as it is in a different region from the first game.

Yes, you can find spoilers about the first game. Of the two returning characters, one will tell you a lot about it. Much of it is technically optional… but she is honestly one of the best parts of AT2, whether you know AT1 or not, and you'll naturally want to know more.

I say screw the chronology and play the GOAT. Try the first one after you've gotten yourself obsessed. And when you do, DO IT FOR **HER**.

## Wait, you never talked about the gameplay!

There's a lot I haven't talked about, but you got me.

If you ask me, Ar tonelico is best understood as a visual novel. The JPRG is designed to lure a broader audience in. It's a story game, through and through. It has ridiculous layers of optional text upon optional text.

I don't think the combat system is bad actually, but you need to understand something. I've played this game around ten times. I don't love being made to participate in the JRPG anymore.

For a first timer, I think you'll probably enjoy it well enough, but be aware that it is quite easy on normal difficulty.

If you're a sicko that likes the JRPG, there's a hard mode that's actually not in the original release that will force you to actually engage with the mechanics.

Anyway, you have two Vanguards and two Reyvateils. The Vanguards protect the Reyvateils as they charge up their powerful attacks, heal, or provide other support. Combat is divided into two phases, Attack and Defense.

<img loading="lazy" src="/media/blog/2026/09/attestation/attack.webp">

For the Attack Phase, you are given a timer to mash as many attacks as possible in. Each direction you can mash comes with different benefits, and they fill up a gauge in the lower right. It's best to mash up first as this unlocks stronger attacks (once you've leveled up enough to have the second and third stage attacks). Stronger attacks have a recharge timer of their own, and they fill out the gauge faster. After mashing up, I would then tend to mash in the direction required for a Reyvateil's Song Magic to evolve to the next stage, this is indicated by a sparkle on the gauge. Finally, I'd recommend mashing according to your preference and understanding of the mechanics. I picked left most of the time, down when I was feeling extra.

<img loading="lazy" src="/media/blog/2026/09/attestation/defense.webp">

In the Defense Phase, you guard the Reyvateils from timed attacks. Doing it successfully reduces or even eliminates damage (when perfect) and increases your burst.

Call me a dweeb, but I do not hold the controller normally playing AT2 during combat because of the Defense Phase so I came up with a custom controller config just for this game. I have trouble mashing Square and X at the same time, as some guard actions require. AT2 doesn't use R2/L2 for anything, so there is space for custom controller mappings if you have a similar skill issue.

Every now and then, enemies get special animations for their attacks. This can be a little problematic, as the visual effects can be distracting, or the camera can shift to a different position. I imagine it's only a particular issue on hard, given how easy the game is… it's mostly an annoyance otherwise.

There are a fair number of mechanics I could not explain to you because you do not need to engage with them on normal or easy. Equipping costumes to your Reyvateils comes with bonuses, but what do the bonuses actually mean? Heck if I know, I don't care. I value the talk topics you get from using costumes more.

(After I wrote this, I actually read all the tutorial text. I emphasize that I may now know, but still do not care.)

## To those of you who have played an earlier translation

If there is love for Ar tonelico 2 within you, do yourself the favor of replaying the game with this new translation.

It's incredibly easy to skewer the NISA release. Everybody knows that version sucks. Conversely, I do not wish to speak of the 2016 retranslation as if it is bad; I think it's more accurate to say it did not go far enough.

Project Metafalica 2.0 is the definitive version, there is simply no comparison.

The overall text flows extremely well, and the cast as a whole is now rather articulate. I think it comes off very well in Cloche, who now has real vocabulary and a proper formal presence, which I think can be hard to nail in English. Unfortunate that I can't go over literally every character, but take this as a prompt to see how well their voices are conveyed now.

It is nigh impossible to recapture the feeling of a first time playthrough. It's even worse when you've loved this game as I have. While playing, I thought to myself multiple times that it was a shame that I couldn't recapture the feeling of a first run.

But there was a single part that actually did recapture that feeling. Something that PM 2.0 went out of its way to add in, something that enhances your understanding of that particular part. If you are a fan of Luca, you are in for a treat because you'll understand her song. I sobbed.

It's not the only addition this version has, but it's quite possibly the best among them.

## My final appeal

Ar tonelico 2 asks what it is to know someone, yes. But there are other themes. The first is relationships of all kinds, not just romantic. There are several broken relationships within the game, and much of its focus is on mending those relationships or creating new ones. The reason there are so many broken relationships relates to the core theme – the pursuit of happiness.

Metafalss is a land on the brink, rife with suffering and poverty. And in this hopeless land, through all the misery, it manages to assert *joy* in life.

You are meant to ponder what happiness means to you, and damn if certain characters don't slam down bars about this.

I could tell you what I love about the heroines. They're absolutely rotten and I love them. I could tell you about the crazy parts where I brought popcorn, the times where I wanted to give them hugs, their moments of triumph and growth. I could, but I would like you to see these things for yourself.

I firmly believe that joy is meant to be shared. To me, there is not a game that brings greater bliss than Ar tonelico 2, and I would share that with you. Without exaggeration, no game means more to me, and no piece of fiction has contributed to my happiness more.
